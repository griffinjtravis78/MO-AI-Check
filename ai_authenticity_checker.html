Visual & Textual Tell-Tale Checklist

Click any media category below to expand the inspection guidelines.

While older tricks like counting fingers are less reliable now, image generation still struggles with physics and deep consistency.

  • Content Credentials & Watermarks: Look for cryptographic provenance data (like C2PA standards backed by Adobe, Google, and OpenAI) via tools like contentcredentials.org or hidden metadata watermarks (such as SynthID).
  • Reverse Image Search: Drop the image into Google Lens or TinEye to check where it first appeared or if it’s a real photo pasted with AI elements.
  • Geometric & Physics Flaws: Check background alignment. Look for melting architecture, asymmetrical earrings or eyeglasses, impossible reflections in glass/water, and shadows pointing in multiple directions due to multiple light sources.
  • Garbled Text: Signs, storefronts, and book spines often render as beautiful gibberish or nonsense letters.

AI video generation adds temporal dimensions where things can glitch over time.

  • Morphing Backgrounds: Watch static background elements (like bricks, trees, or crowd members) to see if they subtly shift, warp, or change shape between frames.
  • Anatomical Inconsistencies: Watch human movement for sudden slips in physics, such as hands passing through objects, stiff body language, or a glass/unblinking stare.
  • Audio-Visual Sync: Check if lip movements precisely match speech, or if ambient environmental background noise disappears unnaturally during dialogue.

AI text detectors are notoriously unreliable and prone to false positives (especially on non-native speakers), so human evaluation of style and substance is key.

  • Stylistic Fingerprints: AI writing often relies on predictable structures—such as opening with a broad statement, using an overly balanced, neutral tone, and wrapping up with a mononymous conclusion labeled simply "Conclusion".
  • Overused Jargon & Transitions: Watch for favorite AI vocabulary words like delve, tapestry, align, seamless, testament, and beacon, alongside an over-reliance on em dashes and breathless, short sentences.
  • Fabricated Citations: AI frequently hallucinates fake studies, exact quotes, and non-existent links that look completely authentic until you click through to verify them.

AI-generated code looks clean on the surface but often carries structural tells:

  • The "Too Clever" or Generic Solution: AI tends to write overly verbose, standard textbook algorithms or boilerplate code that solves a problem in an overly complex or abstract way.
  • Phantom Dependencies: Code blocks may import libraries, packages, or specific module functions that do not actually exist or are deprecated.
  • Subtle Logic Gaps: The syntax will often compile and look pristine, but it can miss edge cases, security validation, or state-handling logic native to the broader project architecture.

Verification Best Practices:

  • Always use the original file downloaded straight from the source (avoid screenshots or compressed downloads).
  • Run it through a C2PA verifier (contentcredentials.org) to check for a valid digital signature.
  • Inspect file properties or ExifTool for software tags, descriptions, or generator prompts if C2PA data is absent.
