Visual & Textual Tell-Tale Checklist

Click any category to expand inspection criteria.

While older tricks like counting fingers are less reliable now, image generation still struggles with physics and deep consistency.

  • Content Credentials & Watermarks: Look for cryptographic provenance data (like C2PA standards backed by Adobe, Google, and OpenAI) via tools like contentcredentials.org or hidden metadata watermarks (such as SynthID).
  • Reverse Image Search: Drop the image into Google Lens or TinEye to check where it first appeared or if it’s a real photo pasted with AI elements.
  • Geometric & Physics Flaws: Check background alignment. Look for melting architecture, asymmetrical earrings or eyeglasses, impossible reflections in glass/water, and shadows pointing in multiple directions.
  • Garbled Text: Signs, storefronts, and book spines often render as beautiful gibberish or nonsense letters.

AI video generation adds temporal dimensions where things can glitch over time.

  • Morphing Backgrounds: Watch static background elements (bricks, trees, crowds) to see if they subtly shift, warp, or change shape between frames.
  • Anatomical Inconsistencies: Watch human movement for sudden slips in physics, hands passing through objects, stiff body language, or a glass/unblinking stare.
  • Audio-Visual Sync: Check if lip movements precisely match speech, or if ambient environmental background noise disappears unnaturally during dialogue.

AI text detectors are notoriously unreliable and prone to false positives, so human evaluation of style and substance is key.

  • Stylistic Fingerprints: AI writing relies on predictable structures—opening with a broad statement, neutral tone, and a mononymous conclusion labeled "Conclusion".
  • Overused Jargon & Transitions: Watch for words like delve, tapestry, align, seamless, testament, beacon, robust, leverage, pivotal, and synergy, plus em dashes and breathless short sentences.
  • HR & Resume Screening: Look for generic fluff lacking specific metrics/budgets, unnaturally balanced bullet point rhythms, and unusual PDF metadata authoring tags.
  • Complementary HR Ecosystem Tools:
    • Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS): Uniform parsing via platforms like NEOGOV or Workday to prevent hidden graphic-design layout tricks.
    • Document Forensic Tools: ExifTool or Office property inspectors to check author metadata and creation timestamps.
    • Controlled Assessments: Sandbox platforms (TestGorilla/HackerRank) to establish verified writing and skill baselines.
    • Structured Behavioral Rubrics: Competency-based interview questions to force candidates to verbally defend resume claims.
  • Fabricated Citations: AI frequently hallucinates fake studies, exact quotes, and non-existent links that look completely authentic until verified.

AI-generated code looks clean on the surface but often carries structural tells:

  • The "Too Clever" or Generic Solution: AI tends to write overly verbose, standard textbook algorithms or boilerplate code in an overly complex way.
  • Phantom Dependencies: Code blocks may import libraries, packages, or specific module functions that do not actually exist or are deprecated.
  • Subtle Logic Gaps: Syntax will compile and look pristine, but it can miss edge cases, security validation, or state-handling logic native to project architecture.

Verification Best Practices:
  • Always use the original file downloaded straight from the source (avoid screenshots or compressed downloads).
  • Run it through a C2PA verifier (contentcredentials.org) for digital signatures.
  • Inspect file properties or ExifTool for software tags, descriptions, or generator prompts if C2PA data is absent.
